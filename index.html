Problem Statement

Empirical Need & Local Context

Community college students do not lack ambition; they lack tools that reconcile static academic obligations with highly volatile wage-earning shifts.

The Planning & Follow-Through Dilemma

Community college students operate under tight structural constraints. The American Association of Community Colleges (AACC) reports that 66% of credit students attend part-time, with 59% of them working full-time jobs. Turning 3–4 dense, inconsistently formatted syllabi into a viable weekly plan requires high cognitive overhead.

When a retail or healthcare shift is rescheduled mid-week, pre-built static schedules immediately break. Students are left to recalculate deadlines under pressure, frequently discovering overdue assignments only after penalties have applied.

"An experiment across 9,000+ students at three institutions tested generic weekly scheduling modules and reminders. It found no improvement in credits, grades, or retention. Generating plans or repeat use does not prove value without follow-through on verified dates."
— Oreopoulos et al., NBER Working Paper #25036

Wake Tech Support Structure & Opportunity

Wake Technical Community College organizes student support through dedicated career-field Care Teams (such as the Education Care Team), pairing students with academic advisors, success coaches, and conversational AI agents such as Edai.

However, current college AI agents focus strictly on transactional Q&A (e.g., financial aid dates, registration steps). None parse course syllabi, verify individual assignment deadlines, or reconstruct weekly study calendars when job shifts change.

The CampusCompass Opportunity: Add an adaptive weekly planning and pedagogical routine directly into existing Care Team workflows, enabling students to arrive at human advising appointments with organized, current plans and specific academic questions.

Technical Specification

System Architecture: Two Connected Workflows

A repeatable cycle that handles date verification, constraint-based planning, and instructor-aligned learning assistance.

Phase 01

Ingest & Availability

Students upload permitted syllabi or schedule excerpts alongside non-academic obligations and available hours. Personal circumstances remain private.

Phase 02

Attribution & Verification

AI extracts assignment names and deadlines, citing exact document passages and page numbers. Only student-confirmed dates enter the calendar.

Phase 03

Constraint Scheduling

Calculates manageable study blocks across courses, preserving buffers and flagging overload. Instructor deadlines remain inviolate.

Phase 04

Dynamic Adaptation

When an employer changes a work shift, the engine detects collisions, isolates unfinished work, and re-allocates sessions with one-click approval.

Phase 05

Pedagogical Coaching

Delivers Socratic hints and practice comparisons within instructor policy bounds. Out-of-scope needs route immediately to Care Team advisors.

User Personas

Who Will Benefit from the Solution

Engineered specifically for students who already possess clear educational goals but require systematic support executing on them.

Cohort 01

Working Students

Employed in retail, food service, or healthcare with variable shift rosters. When shift times move, the engine detects conflicts, preserves buffers, and drafts instructor notices before deadlines lapse.

Impact: Reduces unexcused late submissions caused by last-minute workplace scheduling.
Cohort 02

First-Semester Students

Navigating collegiate systems, multiple LMS interfaces, and syllabus terminology for the first time. Provides a single verified timeline with source links, fostering independent time management habits early.

Impact: Lowers early withdrawal friction and teaches self-directed planning habits.
Cohort 03

Returning Adult Learners

Balancing academic re-entry with dependent care and career transitions. Facilitates 30- to 45-minute study intervals without requiring disclosure of personal or family circumstances.

Impact: Structured scaffolding adaptable to non-traditional and parenting schedules.
Institutional

Care Team Staff & Advisors

Wake Tech academic advisors and coaches operate under 30-minute appointment limits. Students arrive with verified plans and clear questions, shifting meeting time from calendar triage to high-value guidance.

Impact: Estimated 12–24 hours saved over pilot in routine logistical triage.
Governance Framework

Responsible by Design: Safety, Privacy & FERPA

Every anticipated failure mode is bound to a hard system control, human oversight protocol, and immediate institutional pause authority.

Risk Domain Technical Architecture Control Human Oversight Protocol
Date Hallucinations & Errors All dates display explicit document and page citations. Ambiguous dates stay unscheduled. Mandatory student verification is required before dates enter calendars. Staff audit 2 course extractions per week; critical extraction bugs trigger immediate feature pause.
Privacy & FERPA Protection Zero direct integration with Student Information Systems (SIS). Data ingestion is limited strictly to syllabi and user-declared hours. Semester-long retention with full student deletion controls. Wake Tech platform administrators control access permissions and retention schedules.
Academic Dishonesty & Solvers Agent strictly enforces course-level policies. Zero answers provided on graded lab work, quizzes, or exams. Operates exclusively in Socratic hint mode. Referral routing directly to course instructors and Wake Tech Academic Success Center tutoring.
Algorithmic Bias & Inequity Validated across non-traditional schedules (third-shift, rotating weekend rosters). Accessible plain-language output; keyboard and screen-reader compliant. Care Teams evaluate demographic distribution of volunteers to verify equitable utility.
Over-Reliance & De-skilling Explicit disclosure of AI assistance. Scaffolding systematically fades: reminder frequency is student-governed, and weekly check-ins prompt independent schedule drafting. Independent planning capability scores evaluated at baseline and pilot conclusion.
Institutional Control Configured on college-approved agent environments (e.g. Element451 Bolt) with complete enterprise auditing and access boundaries. Institutional Kill-Switch: Sponsoring Care Team holds unilateral authority to pause or terminate the pilot.
Deployment Schedule

Staged Pilot Roadmap & Quantitative Benchmarks

A 4-phase rollout protocol from December 2026 to June 2027 with pre-agreed evaluation gates.

Phase 1 • Dec 2026 – Jan 2027
Institutional Discovery
  • Confirm sponsoring Care Team
  • Conduct 5–8 student and 2–3 staff interviews
  • Audit existing planning tool capabilities
  • Verify platform licensing and data boundaries
Phase 2 • Feb – Mar 2027
Configuration & Stress Testing
  • Configure workflow on approved platform
  • Run synthetic test suite across messy syllabi
  • Validate shift conflict handling algorithms
  • Secure formal Care Team approval before launch
Phase 3 • Spring 2027
Live Student Cohort
  • Recruit 15–25 volunteers across 1–2 Care Teams
  • Record 2-week pre-pilot baseline metrics
  • Conduct weekly extraction audits (2 syllabi/wk)
  • Administer weekly follow-through check-ins
Phase 4 • May – Jun 2027
Decision Gate & Expansion
  • Evaluate reliability, independence, and costs
  • Review staff effort totals against budget
  • Conduct formal Go / No-Go expansion review
  • Publish complete pilot findings and recommendations

Proposed Quantitative Decision Thresholds

Usage alone does not justify expansion; targets are defined upfront with denominators.

≥95% Extraction Precision & Recall: Verified across audited syllabus dates without invented or missing milestones.
75% Exit Usefulness: Minimum percentage of participants rating utility 4 or 5 out of 5.
-20% Planning Friction: Reduction in median weekly minutes spent compiling schedules across courses.
≥60% On-Time Milestone Rate: Participants demonstrating a 10 percentage point gain or maintaining ≥90%.
